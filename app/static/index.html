01 / ANCIENT WISDOM FROM SUPERCONDUCTING QUBITS

True Quantum Oracle

Consult Unseen Forces ↗

Can consciousness and intention influence the quantum field? We're not sure, but gnosis.bot makes it possible to ask this question by actually taking hardware readings of the quantum field and documenting any anomalies that might arise.

The Quantum I Ching Oracle honors the traditional six-line, three-coin I Ching casting, but adds a modern twist. The coin tosses and changing-line calculations are represented by actual qubits.

I developed and tested circuits with Qiskit on IBM quantum hardware, measuring coin states and independently checking the additional quantum moving-line flags. The readings retain the traditional Now → Becoming structure, and the interface makes its physical measurements and their imperfections visible.

How quantum computing happens using IBM's cloud-based quantum platform

  1. gnosis.bot sends instructions to IBM's system through a cloud interface.
  2. Classical computers translate those instructions into control signals using digital-to-analog conversion (DAC), which pass through microwave electronics.
  3. Microwave pulses travel into the cryogenic environment through superconducting coaxial cables.
  4. After the quantum chip processes the instructions, the output signals return through readout loops.
  5. Isolators in the readout chain help prevent unwanted signals from reflecting back toward the qubits.
  6. Quantum amplifiers strengthen the very weak readout signals.
  7. Room-temperature electronics digitize those signals with analog-to-digital conversion (ADC) and analyze the resulting measurements.
  8. The instrument processes the measurement data into the six-line reading and audits the moving-line flags.
  9. The resulting Now and Becoming hexagrams return to gnosis.bot for display and interpretation.

Hardware sequence adapted from Introduction to Superconducting Quantum Computers, written by Peir-Ru Wang. The final presentation and interpretation steps describe gnosis.bot's application of the process.
