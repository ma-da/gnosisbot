QUANTUM ARCHIVE · METHODOLOGY

Finding representative quantum readings

This visualization explores 128 measurements recorded during an IBM quantum computer experiment. Each measurement contains the outcomes of a six-line I Ching casting.

What is a medoid?

A medoid is an actual observation that best represents a group of observations. Unlike a mathematical average, it must be one of the original measured readings.

medoid(C) = arg minx∈C ∑y∈C d(x,y)

For each candidate reading x in cluster C, calculate its total distance from the other readings y. The reading with the smallest total is a medoid.

How the readings are grouped

The 128 archived quantum measurements are grouped into similarity clusters. Differences in yin/yang polarity contribute two distance units per line, while differences in changing-line status contribute one.

Each cluster is represented by its medoid: the measured reading with the smallest total distance to the other readings in that cluster.

How the featured reading is selected

The featured demonstration is ranked separately. The ranking emphasizes agreement between the physically measured QPU moving flags and the coin-derived results, rewards readings with a manageable number of changing lines, and considers cluster size and average similarity.

This selection procedure applies only to the archived demonstration. It is not used to choose a visitor's fresh or privately allocated quantum reading.

These are historical physical quantum measurements, not predictions or probabilities of future readings.
